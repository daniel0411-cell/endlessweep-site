## Accuracy comes before speed

Fast play begins with deductions that feel automatic. Guessing early, correcting wrong flags, and rescanning the same clues cost more time than a short verification routine.

## Use a consistent scan direction

Move around the open boundary in one direction. Finish the certain moves in one area, then continue. A consistent scan reduces duplicate work and helps you notice newly solvable clues.

## Recognize complete clue states

Train two immediate responses: when a clue has all its mines flagged, reveal its other neighbors; when all remaining covered neighbors must be mines, flag them. Pattern practice builds this recognition without requiring a full board.

## Use chord clicks carefully

Chord reveals several squares with one action. It is one of the largest speed gains available, but only when the flags are correct. Read the [chord guide](/minesweeper-chord/) and practice on Beginner before relying on it on Expert.

## Reduce unnecessary flags

You do not need to flag every mine before winning. Flag when it preserves information, enables a chord, or prevents a mistake. Extra flag and unflag actions lower Endlessweep Efficiency.

## Understand Endlessweep Efficiency

Endlessweep Efficiency is the percentage of recorded actions that advance the board. A reveal or chord counts as progress when it opens at least one new square. Flag and unflag actions do not count as progress. This is a lightweight local measure, not standard 3BV efficiency.

## Practice one skill at a time

- Beginner: pattern recognition and clean controls.
- Intermediate: scanning and overlapping clues.
- Expert: sustained concentration and choosing where to work next.
- Pattern Practice: fast recognition of specific arrangements.

## Review your replay

Use Replay to find long pauses, repeated flag changes, and missed chord opportunities. Compare recent games on the Statistics page, but judge trends across several games because board difficulty varies.
