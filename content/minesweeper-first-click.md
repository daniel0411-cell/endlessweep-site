## Is the first click safe?

That depends on the game. Endlessweep protects the first clicked square and its immediate neighbors when placing mines, so the opening cannot immediately hit a mine and usually reveals an area. Other Minesweeper versions may protect only the clicked square or use a pre-generated board.

## Center, edge or corner?

A center click has more neighboring squares and may create a larger opening, but more neighbors do not guarantee a better board. An edge or corner produces fewer adjacent clues, which can make early constraints easier to read.

> There is no universal first square that guarantees a win. The best opening depends on the version's board-generation rules and the mine arrangement created after the click.

## A practical Endlessweep opening

On Beginner, start away from the outermost edge to expose several clues. On Expert, choose a visible central area that leaves room to work in multiple directions. After the opening, stop thinking about location and follow the revealed information.

## Does the first click change a seeded board?

Yes. Endlessweep derives the mine layout from the seed, difficulty, dimensions, mine count, and first-click position. Shared challenges reproduce the same board only when the other player uses the same first square.

## Common first-click myths

- A corner is not always safer after the first move.
- The center does not guarantee the largest opening.
- A large opening does not guarantee an easy finish.
- First-click safety does not guarantee a no-guess solution.

## What to do after the opening

Scan the complete boundary of the newly opened area. First identify clues whose mines are already accounted for, then identify clues whose remaining covered neighbors must all be mines. Continue with the [complete winning routine](/how-to-win-minesweeper/).

## When the opening is small

A single number or narrow opening can still be playable. Avoid clicking randomly beside it. If the clue does not force a move, inspect whether another protected opening rule applies; otherwise compare risks using the [guessing guide](/minesweeper-guessing/).
