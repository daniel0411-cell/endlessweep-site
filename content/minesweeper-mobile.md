## Mobile Minesweeper basics

The rules do not change on a phone: reveal every safe square, read the number clues, and flag proven mines. The main difference is that a touch screen does not have separate left and right mouse buttons.

## Reveal and Flag modes

Endlessweep shows a mode button on narrow screens. In **Reveal** mode, tapping a covered square opens it. In **Flag** mode, tapping places or removes a flag. Check the mode label before every precise move near a mine.

## Press and hold to flag

You can also press and hold a covered square to place or remove a flag. After the long press fires, lift your finger without making a second tap. Use the visible Flag mode when repeated long presses feel slow or unreliable on your device.

## How to chord on mobile

Switch to Reveal mode and tap an already open number. If the adjacent flag count matches the clue, the game reveals the other covered neighbors. Verify all flags first because a wrong flag can make the chord open a mine.

## Playing large boards

Intermediate and Expert boards can be wider than the screen. The board sits in a horizontal scrolling area, so drag across the surrounding board area to reach other sections. Work on one frontier at a time rather than repeatedly jumping across the entire board.

## Prevent accidental taps

- Keep the current mode visible before acting.
- Zoom is unnecessary; the game uses stable square sizes and horizontal scrolling.
- Pause before switching apps so the timer does not continue.
- Use Flag mode for dense sections with several proven mines.
- Switch back to Reveal mode before tapping open numbers to chord.

## Best difficulty for a phone

Beginner fits most screens and is best for learning touch controls. Intermediate provides deeper logic while remaining manageable through scrolling. Expert is fully playable but benefits from landscape orientation and deliberate section-by-section scanning.

## Continue on another device

Statistics and replays are stored in the current browser, so they do not automatically sync between phone and desktop. A shared seed URL can reproduce a challenge, but the same first-click position is also required.
