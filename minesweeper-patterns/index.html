How pattern solving works

A pattern is useful because neighboring clues describe many of the same hidden squares. Compare the clues as a group, then mark only the squares the numbers prove.

The 1-2-1 pattern

121

In the common 1-2-1 arrangement, the outer hidden squares are often mines and the middle hidden square is safe, when the three clues share the same unrevealed row or column. Always check the surrounding board before applying it.

The 2-1-2 pattern

212

The 2-1-2 arrangement is the reverse-style deduction: with the same shared hidden squares and no extra flags changing the count, the outer positions can be safe while the middle positions account for the mines. Treat the neighboring cells as part of the proof.

The 1-2-2 pattern

122

For 1-2-2 and similar patterns, compare the extra hidden squares on the side of the larger clue. The difference between the clues can identify a mine or a safe square without guessing.

Patterns are shortcuts for logic, not magic shapes. Rotate or mirror them, and verify that no additional hidden or flagged square changes the numbers.
